02 — Copy rules

What the security actually is, and what the foreman is told instead.
What happensWhat we do not sayWhat the foreman reads
A key pair is generated in the Android Keystore, backed by the device's trusted execution environment. “Generating hardware-backed key in TEE” Creating this phone's safety lock
The private key is non-exportable and never leaves the secure element. “Non-exportable private key” The lock stays inside this phone. It cannot be copied to another phone.
The public key is registered against the employee record as the authorised signer. “Registering public key / enrolling device certificate” Registering the phone with HR
Each attendance record is signed on-device, so the server can verify origin and detect tampering. “Records are cryptographically signed” Your records carry your name. Nobody else can send attendance in your name.
Signing works without connectivity; verification happens at sync. “Offline-capable asymmetric signing” It works with no signal.
Device revocation and re-enrolment are administered by HR. “Revoke the device credential” If you lose this phone, tell HR. They will register your new phone and switch this one off.
Say the benefit, not the mechanism
“Nobody else can send attendance in your name” is the same fact as a signature chain, in words a foreman can repeat to his crew.
No decision to make
The step has no options, no consent dialog, no settings. It runs, it reports, it hands him a roll call button.
One padlock, no more
A single quiet lock glyph and a shield on success. Security theatre — badges, scanning animations, hex strings — reads as a scam to a first-time user.
Failure names the fix, not the fault
No signal is the likely failure at 06:30 on a site. The screen gives three physical things to try and a phone number, not an error code.
