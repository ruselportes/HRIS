How Cryptographic Anti-Tampering Works

An easy-to-understand breakdown of how our mobile app catches fake attendance timestamps—even when offline with no internet connection!

💡 The Main Problem in Simple Terms

Imagine a site foreman at a remote construction site with no cell service. He opens his phone settings, rolls the clock back from 9:00 AM to 7:00 AM, and marks a worker as clocking in early.

Standard apps rely on the phone's clock and get tricked. Our app uses 4 hardware & cryptographic layers to catch the trick instantly.

❌ What a Cheater Tries to Do

1. Goes to phone Settings ➔ Date & Time.
2. Turns off "Automatic Time" and sets the time back 2 hours.
3. Clocks in late workers so they look punctual.
4. Expects the HR server to accept the fake times when syncing later.

🛡️ How the System Catches Them

1. The app completely ignores the phone's clock.
2. It uses an un-changeable internal hardware stopwatch.
3. It seals records with tamper-evident digital locks.
4. The server validates signatures and rejects altered records!

🔒 The 4 Layers of Protection Explained Simply

1
The Hardware Stopwatch (Monotonic Clock)
💡 Real-World Analogy: Think of a marathon runner wearing a sealed physical stopwatch that started counting seconds the moment the race started. Even if the runner turns back their arm wrist-watch display, the internal stopwatch keeps ticking continuously!
Instead of asking the phone "What time is it?" (which users can change), the app asks the phone chip: elapsedRealtime()—meaning "How many milliseconds have passed since this phone turned on?". Users CANNOT change or pause this counter without restarting the device hardware.
2
Time Calibration (Anchor & Offset Math)
💡 Real-World Analogy: Before you hike into a remote canyon with no signal, you sync your stopwatch with the official town clock at 8:00 AM (Stopwatch at 1,000 sec). 5 hours later (Stopwatch at 19,000 sec), you know the true time is 1:00 PM simply by adding the 5-hour difference!
The last time the app was online, it saved an Anchor Point: True Server Time + Monotonic Stopwatch Reading. When logging attendance offline, true event time is computed by relative math:
True Event Time = Saved Server Anchor Time + (Current Stopwatch - Saved Anchor Stopwatch)
3
Tamper-Evident Ledger Chain (HMAC-SHA256)
💡 Real-World Analogy: Imagine a numbered paper checkbook where every page contains a unique wax seal fingerprint made from the previous page's contents. If someone tears out page #3 or edits a number on page #2, the seal on page #4 stops matching!
Every time attendance is logged, the record is locked together with the previous record's cryptographic hash key into an append-only chain. If a user hacks into the phone database to edit past records, the hash chain breaks instantly upon sync.
4
Unforgeable Hardware Stamp (TEE / Secure Enclave)
💡 Real-World Analogy: Like an official government notary seal stamped directly by a physical security vault inside the phone. Software, apps, and even root hackers cannot steal or forge this seal.
Before syncing data to the web HRIS server, the mobile device signs the attendance payload inside the phone's hardware security chip (Android Keystore / iOS Secure Enclave) using ECDSA asymmetric cryptography. The server verifies this signature to confirm the payload came from an authentic, registered device.
